# Offers: the reasons a stranger raises their hand

Self-generated pipeline needs a low-friction first step that gives real value before any money
or meeting is discussed. These are the Phase 1 offers. Each one is a rung on the ladder from
reader to client.

## The ladder

```
  free & instant        free & personal          paid & scoped
  Scorecard        ->   Revenue Leak Review   ->  consulting engagement
  (self-serve)          (30-min call)             (audit, project, retainer)
```

## 1. The Revenue Leak Scorecard (live)

- **What:** a free, 3-minute self-assessment at `/revenue-scorecard.html`. Eight questions on
  CRM data, lead response, pipeline stages, forecasting, process, handoffs, reporting and
  follow-up. Returns a score out of 24, a band, and the three biggest leaks.
- **Why it works:** it is useful on its own, it is specific to Lauren's expertise, and it makes
  the visitor self-diagnose a problem she is the obvious person to fix. The result is captured
  with the lead, so every scorecard lead arrives graded.
- **Capture payload:** `offer`, `source`, `score`, `band`, `answers`, plus name, work email,
  company, team size and an optional message.
- **The hook to the next rung:** the results page and the first response both offer the Revenue
  Leak Review as the natural way to turn the score into a plan.

## 2. The Revenue Leak Review (the tripwire consult)

- **What:** a focused 30-minute call. Lauren reads the scorecard result back, confirms the two
  or three leaks worth fixing first, and gives a plain next step. No slides, no pitch marathon.
- **Who it is offered to:** grade A and B leads, in the first response draft. Grade C is offered
  it once they have engaged with the follow-up.
- **Why it works:** it is a small, specific yes. It moves the deal from New Enquiry to Discovery
  Scheduled and lets Lauren qualify properly in person.
- **Booking:** Lauren sends her scheduling link, or the engine drafts a couple of concrete time
  options for Lauren to confirm. The engine never books on a prospect's behalf without approval.

## 3. Consulting engagement (the paid work)

The Review leads into scoped work that maps to the service pages:
- CRM audit, selection, implementation, adoption
- Revenue operations: process mapping, SOPs, pipeline management, forecasting, dashboards
- Conversion and funnel optimisation
- Hospitality tech market entry and fractional sales leadership

Pricing and scoping are Lauren's call, per deal. The engine prepares context and drafts, it
does not quote.

## Positioning notes (for the Scribe)

- Lead with the prospect's own result, not with The Pearson Co. The scorecard already earned
  the right to a reply; use it.
- Be specific about the leak and what it tends to cost, in plain terms. No fear-selling, no
  inflated figures.
- One clear next step per message. Usually: read the breakdown, then a short call.
- Warm, direct, human. Lauren's voice, house style. British English, no em dashes, no jargon.

## Phase 2 offers (documented, not live)

When Lauren switches on outbound, the same ladder applies, with a cold-friendly top rung: a
short, specific, personalised note that offers the scorecard or a single relevant insight,
never a hard pitch. See `roles/the-prospector.md`.
