Conversion & Funnel Optimisation

Free CRO tools worth using. No budget required to find the fix.

Lauren PearsonFounder, The Pearson Co.23 October 20267 min read

Written by Lauren Pearson, Founder of The Pearson Co., a commercial growth and conversion consultant. More on her background at the about section.

The free CRO tools worth using are Microsoft Clarity for heatmaps and session recordings, Google Search Console for landing page query data, PageSpeed Insights for speed problems, and the exploration reports built into Google Analytics 4 for funnel drop-off. Run all four on your highest-traffic page first, fix what they show you, then decide whether you still need to pay for anything.

Why this matters. Most teams don't have the traffic to justify paid tools yet.

Conversion rate optimisation has a reputation for needing a testing budget, a dedicated analyst and a tool stack that costs more per month than a small team spends on hosting for a year. For most founder-led businesses, that reputation is simply wrong. Baymard Institute's aggregated cart abandonment research, drawn from fifty separate studies and last updated in September 2025, puts the average documented online shopping cart abandonment rate at 70.22 percent. That number has barely moved in years, which tells you something useful: most of the conversion lost on a typical site isn't a subtle problem that needs a sophisticated test to find. It's a visible, fixable piece of friction that a free diagnostic tool will show you in an afternoon.

The tools below cost nothing, need no engineering time to install beyond a single tracking snippet, and between them cover the three questions that matter before you spend a penny on anything paid: where are visitors actually struggling on the page, which search queries bring people in only for them to leave, and is the page itself slow enough to be losing people before they've read a word.

The list, with how to apply each. Four tools, one afternoon.

Microsoft Clarity. Free since its 2020 launch, with no traffic cap and no sampling, Clarity records real visitor sessions and builds heatmaps from them. Install the snippet on your highest-traffic landing page or checkout flow first, not your homepage. Watch ten to fifteen session recordings before drawing any conclusion; one recording is an anecdote, ten start to show a pattern. Look specifically for rage clicks, repeated clicking on something that isn't responding, which is usually the single fastest friction point to find and fix.

Google Search Console. Once verified, it's free for any site and shows exactly which search queries bring visitors to each page, along with the click-through rate and average position for that query. Use it to spot pages ranking for the right query but converting badly, which points to a messaging mismatch between what the searcher expected and what the page delivers, rather than a traffic problem.

PageSpeed Insights. Google's free Core Web Vitals report scores a page on load speed and interactivity, and flags the specific element slowing it down, usually an oversized image or a render-blocking script. Page speed is one of the most underrated conversion levers precisely because it's invisible in a standard analytics dashboard; a visitor who leaves after two seconds on a slow-loading page doesn't show up as a conversion problem, just as a bounce.

Google Analytics 4 exploration reports. The free tier of GA4 includes funnel exploration, which lets you build a simple step-by-step view of where visitors drop between, say, product page, cart and checkout, without paying for a dedicated funnel analytics tool. It won't tell you why someone dropped off the way a session recording will, which is exactly why it's worth running alongside Clarity rather than instead of it.

What the free tools can't do. And why that's fine at this stage.

Google retired its free split-testing tool on 30 September 2023, after announcing the sunset that January. Plenty of older "best free CRO tools" lists online still recommend that now-defunct product, which is worth knowing before you follow one. In practice this matters less than it sounds, because most founder-led sites don't have anywhere near the monthly traffic a proper split test needs to reach statistical significance within a reasonable timeframe. Running an underpowered test and acting on the result is worse than not testing at all, because it produces false confidence rather than a real answer.

The honest substitute at this stage isn't a cheaper testing tool. It's sequencing: diagnose with the free tools above, make the fix, then watch the same metric for a few weeks before and after, on the whole page rather than a fifty-fifty split. It's a cruder method than a controlled test, but it's a method you can actually run on real-world traffic levels, and it still beats guessing.

Where teams go wrong. Collecting data, acting on none of it.

The failure mode I see most often with founder-led teams isn't a lack of data. It's the opposite: Clarity installed, Search Console verified, GA4 configured, and nobody has watched a single session recording in three months. A founder-led ecommerce retailer I worked with had exactly this setup running for most of a year before anyone opened Clarity properly. When we did, the first ten recordings on the product listing page showed the same pattern repeatedly: visitors on mobile tapping a size filter that visually looked selectable but didn't register the tap, then leaving within seconds of the failed tap. It was a single CSS issue, fixed in under a day, that had been sitting in plain sight in session recordings nobody had watched.

The decision rule I now apply with every client before they spend a penny on a paid CRO tool or testing platform is simple: don't commission anything paid until the free diagnostic layer has surfaced at least one concrete, visible friction point you've fixed. If it hasn't surfaced anything after a proper look, four to eight weeks, watching recordings and checking the reports weekly, that's not evidence you've exhausted what's wrong with the page. It's usually evidence nobody is actually looking.

Getting started. Pick one page, run the full stack on it.

Don't spread these four tools thinly across every page on the site in week one. Pick the single page carrying the most traffic relative to its importance, usually a checkout flow, a key landing page or a pricing page, and run all four tools on it together. Within two to three weeks you should have at least one fix worth making. Make it, then watch the same page for another few weeks before moving to the next one. Our guide to using heatmaps for CRO covers how to read session recordings without jumping to conclusions from a handful of sessions, and our conversion rate optimisation work usually starts with exactly this free diagnostic pass before any paid tool or test enters the conversation.

If the free layer surfaces more friction than your team can fix in a reasonable time, that's the point at which a structured CRO engagement earns its cost, prioritising the fixes by impact and getting a backlog of genuine, observed problems actioned rather than guessed at.

Common questions.

What are the best free CRO tools for a small team?

Microsoft Clarity for heatmaps and session recordings, Google Search Console for query-level landing page performance, PageSpeed Insights for speed issues that quietly suppress conversion, and the built-in exploration reports inside Google Analytics 4 for funnel drop-off. None of them cost anything, and together they usually surface more fixable friction than a small team can act on in a month.

Can I still use Google's old free A/B testing tool?

No. Google shut its free split-testing product and its paid tier down on 30 September 2023, after announcing the sunset that January. Any older guide that still lists it as a free testing tool is out of date. Most founder-led sites do not have the traffic to run a statistically sound split test anyway, which is exactly why the diagnostic tools below matter more at this stage than testing software.

Do I need a developer to use these free CRO tools?

Not for the first pass. Microsoft Clarity, Google Search Console and PageSpeed Insights are all set up through a single tracking snippet or domain verification, with no engineering time beyond that. Acting on what they show you, fixing a broken filter or a slow-loading page, is where a developer eventually gets involved, but the diagnosis itself needs none.

How long should I run free CRO tools before I buy a paid one?

Long enough to have fixed at least two or three concrete friction points the free tools surfaced, usually four to eight weeks for a site with modest traffic. Paying for a testing platform before you have exhausted the free diagnostic layer tends to produce tests built on guesses rather than on anything you actually watched a visitor struggle with.
