Customer Journey Mapping

Free customer journey mapping tools. What the free tier actually includes, and when it isn't enough.

Lauren PearsonFounder, The Pearson Co.12 October 20267 min read

The free tiers of customer journey mapping tools such as Miro and UXPressia give you a canvas, templates and basic collaboration at no cost, which covers a single mapping workshop well. Where they stop is behavioural data, a library for several live maps, and stakeholder permissions, which is when a paid plan or a focused template earns its cost instead.

The short answer. Free covers one map done properly.

I'm Lauren Pearson, and when a client asks which tool they need for customer journey mapping, my honest answer is usually: probably none, at least not yet. The tool was rarely the bottleneck on the maps I have seen go wrong. The research behind the map was. That said, a free tool is still worth choosing deliberately, because the free tiers of the main options differ enough to matter once you know what you are using the map for.

This piece compares what the free versions of the common options actually include. If you want a map built without any software account at all, our free customer journey map template is the simplest starting point. If you are weighing whether to go further than a free tool and bring in behavioural data instead of workshop recall, AI customer journey mapping covers that separately, because it is a different question from "which free tool," not a bigger version of the same one.

How they differ. Three kinds of free option.

General whiteboard tools, used for journey mapping. Miro is the obvious example here, and its free plan is genuinely useful for this purpose: an infinite canvas, a library of ready-made journey mapping templates, and real-time collaboration so a team can fill one in together on a call. At the time of writing, paid plans start from around $8 per member per month, which only becomes worth paying once a team needs more boards, guest permissions or integrations than the free plan allows. What Miro's free tier does not give you is anything journey-specific: no persona fields, no built-in touchpoint structure, just a blank canvas and a template to start from.

Dedicated journey mapping tools with a free tier. UXPressia is the clearest example. Its free plan, also $0 at the time of writing, includes a library of more than a hundred journey and persona templates with structured fields built for this exact job, plus real-time collaboration. Its paid plans start at a noticeably higher monthly cost than a general whiteboard tool, aimed at teams running several maps across an organisation rather than one team doing one workshop. The free tier is the right starting point for almost everyone; the paid tier only earns its cost once you are maintaining more maps than the free limits comfortably allow.

A plain template, no software at all. A spreadsheet or slide deck costs nothing, needs no account, and is completely yours to edit. Our own free customer journey map template tracks four stages against the real question a customer is asking at each one, and takes under an hour to fill in properly from real conversations. For a single map done once, this is usually the fastest route to something genuinely useful, not just visually tidy.

Which you need and when. A short decision guide.

  • One-off workshop, small team: a plain template or Miro's free canvas, either works well.
  • Several maps, multiple personas or departments: start on UXPressia's free tier before paying for anything, and only upgrade once you hit its limits.
  • You need real behaviour, not workshop recall: that is no longer a free-tool question. See our piece on AI customer journey mapping instead.

How Lauren would decide. The tool was never the real bottleneck.

Here's a worked scenario, illustrative rather than a specific client result, that shows my actual decision rule in practice. An eight-person hospitality technology team wanted to map their guest booking journey for the first time and asked which paid platform to buy before they had mapped anything at all. My answer was to not buy anything yet. We ran the first mapping workshop on a free template, built entirely from booking support transcripts and three short guest interviews rather than from what the team assumed guests experienced. That map immediately showed a confirmation-email gap nobody on the team had previously flagged, found using a free spreadsheet and real conversations, not a paid feature.

The decision rule I give every team asking this question: start with the free template or a free canvas and run the mapping workshop against real customer or support data before spending anything on software. The tool is rarely what makes a journey map useful. Only once a team is actively maintaining more than two or three live maps across different personas, and needs them kept current and shared properly rather than redrawn from scratch each quarter, does a paid, dedicated platform start to earn its monthly cost.

Common questions.

Is Miro really free for customer journey mapping?

Yes, for the parts that matter in a single workshop. Miro's free plan includes an infinite canvas, journey mapping templates and real-time collaboration, with paid plans starting from around $8 per member per month at the time of writing for teams that need more boards or permissions.

What does UXPressia's free plan include?

A no-cost tier with access to its template library and real-time collaboration, built specifically for journey and persona mapping rather than a general whiteboard. Its paid plans, aimed at teams managing several maps across an organisation, start at a meaningfully higher monthly cost, so most small teams should start on the free tier.

Do I need special software to map a customer journey?

No. A simple spreadsheet or slide template, filled in from real customer conversations, produces a usable map with no account and no cost. Software earns its place once you are maintaining several maps across different personas or departments, not before.

What is missing from free customer journey mapping tools?

Behavioural data pulled automatically from your website, CRM or support tickets, a library that holds multiple live maps cleanly, and fine-grained permissions for larger stakeholder groups. A free tier handles one team running one or two maps well; it is not built for an organisation running many at once.

When should a team pay for a dedicated journey mapping tool?

Once it is maintaining more than two or three live maps across different personas or departments, and needs those maps kept current, shared and permissioned properly rather than redrawn from scratch each time. Before that point, a free tool or template is usually the better spend.
